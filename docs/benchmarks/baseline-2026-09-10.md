compared only the five shipping-binary ids
(`binary/size_bytes`, `yach/tui_startup_first_output_pty`,
`yach/tui_ready_startup_first_output_pty`,
`yach/cli_startup_first_output`, `memory/peak_rss/tui_ready`); every
other row was `no_base_worker`.

Live-terminal `terminal/*` rows were `requires tty` skipped in the
non-TTY `just perf-record` written to
`~/.cache/yach/perf/a1eba7588fa53470/2026-09-10-c2533ee.json`. Those
rows were re-recorded under `script -q /dev/null -- /tmp/perf-tty.sh`
into `/tmp/perf-tty.json` and merged into this report. All other rows
come from the non-TTY `just perf-record` run.

The host fingerprint reports 6 cores on a 12-core CPU. The working
copy was dirty when recorded. `terminal/async_backlog_stress_keypress_to_draw_flush_live`
has a 13.04 s max against a 63.45 µs p95 (one outlier sample).

First-output A/B budgets are taken from the accepted `just perf` document
`.perf/results/20260910T123936-ab.json` by the rule `ceil(spread) + 3`
on that run's base spread: `yach/*_first_output_pty` = 16 (widest PTY
row 12.13%, median deltas −1.19% / +1.43%),
`yach/cli_startup_first_output` = 6 (spread 2.84%, median delta −4.05%).
The glob is `*_first_output_pty` so `yach/tui_startup_profile/..._pty`
rows keep the 5% default rather than inheriting the PTY first-output
budget. Earlier 21–25% / 14.3% spreads were from runs before
child-process workloads were ordered first and are not used.

first-output samplers have round-to-round base spread (10.5–20.3%) above
their evidence-derived budgets (6% / 16%), while every median delta stayed
inside budget (−6.0% to +1.9%). That is documented `inconclusive`, not a
regression. Reducing first-output sampler variance is tracked on
